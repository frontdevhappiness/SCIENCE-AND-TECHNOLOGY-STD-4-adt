Boiling point of water

Water boils when it reaches a specific temperature. The boiling point is the exact temperature at which a liquid (water) boils and turns to water vapour. The boiling point is important because it ensures safe drinking water. Some foods are cooked better when the water has reached its boiling point.

Experiment 6:
Investigating the boiling point of water
Aim:
To demonstrate boiling point of water
Materials:
Heat source (stove), water, saucepan and thermometer
Note:
Do not remove the thermometer from the water while reading the temperature.

Procedure

1.
Place the saucepan with water on the stove that is already switched on.
2.
Measure the temperature of the water as it begins to boil.
3.
Continue measuring the temperature of the water until it boils vigorously.
4.
Record the results.

Results

When water reaches 100 °C (212 °F), it boils. This temperature is known as the boiling point of water.

Conclusion

The experiment showed that boiling point of water is 100 °C.
